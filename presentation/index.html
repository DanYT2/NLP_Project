I. Front page · The problem Filed 21 May 2026

Twenty topics, four learning regimes,
one question: how much representation matters.

What is each step up the representational hierarchy actually worth?
By Dan Waititu HSLU NALAPRO · Spring 2026

Twenty classes, several of them near-duplicates (comp.sys.ibm.pc.hardware vs comp.sys.mac.hardware; soc.religion.christian vs talk.religion.misc). We use 20 Newsgroups as a controlled testbed — sparse statistics, dense embeddings, pretrained transformers, billion-parameter LLMs — and measure what each rung up the ladder is worth.

II. The dataset Section A · Page 2

20 Newsgroups, stripped of leakage.

Headers, footers and quoted replies leak the label. Strip them out and the baseline falls from tutorial-grade 90 % into the low 60s.

fetch_20newsgroups(remove=("headers","footers","quotes")). Roughly class-balanced (377–600 docs/class), heavy-tailed length: median ~40 tokens, p95 ~280, max above 6 700.

Class balance

Train-set document counts per class. Mild imbalance — talk.religion.misc and talk.politics.misc are the smallest classes and consistently the hardest to recover.

Document length distribution

Long tail informs the BERT max_length=256 choice — covers ≥95 % of documents without paying for tokens at the extreme.
Why this matters. Stripped-text baselines start at ~63 % rather than the inflated 90 %+ in tutorial code — so every Q1→Q2→Q3 lift is genuine, not spurious.
III. Question 1 · Shallow MLP Section B · Pages 3–4

Shallow MLP, three representations.

One classifier, three inputs. Any accuracy difference is the representation.

A 2-layer MLP (input → 256 → 20), held fixed across word2vec mean-pool, TF-IDF, and mean+max pooled word2vec. The classifier never moves; only the input does.

Accuracy & macro-F1 by representation

TF-IDF wins the group. Adding max-pool to mean-pool hurts: the max dimension is dominated by high-magnitude function tokens.

Trained vs untrained embeddings

t-SNE of 500 tokens at 1 epoch (left) vs 20 (right). Vocab is identical (19 306); topic clusters only emerge with training — the lift is the embedding, not the lexicon.

Design decisions

  • Stopwords kept for word2vec, dropped for TF-IDF. Word2vec needs function words to stabilise the context window; TF-IDF doesn't.
  • Determinism via workers=1 + seed=42. Gensim's multi-threaded training is non-deterministic — the 1-vs-20-epoch comparison would otherwise be meaningless.
  • Mean+max pool as a no-architecture-change probe. The spec required improving Q1 without touching the network. Doubling the input dim with max-pool isolates representation from capacity.
IV. Question 2 · BERT fine-tune Section B · Page 5

BERT-base, fine-tuned end-to-end.

110 M pretrained parameters, same val indices as Q1. The lift is real because the split is identical.

End-to-end fine-tune of bert-base-uncased via HuggingFace Trainer. Same seed=42 as every other stage — apples to apples.

Q1 best (TF-IDF) — → Q2c (best LR) — —

Q2b · baseline confusion

Diagonal-heavy. Residual error concentrates inside the comp.*, politics, and religion sibling clusters.

Q2c · learning-rate sweep

Best LR: 5e-05 — the canonical BERT fine-tuning rate. Larger destabilises pretrained weights; smaller under-trains in three epochs.

Design decisions

  • max_length=256. ~95th percentile of token-length — covers nearly every document without paying for outliers.
  • Index parity with Q1 and Q3. One data.train_val_split(seed=42) call, reused everywhere — same val set throughout.
  • MPS-safe Trainer flags. fp16=bf16=pin_memory=False; load-best on macro-F1, not loss.
V. Question 3 · MLM then fine-tune Section C · Page 6

MLM pretraining, then fine-tune.

Two stages; Q2b's hyperparameters held verbatim in stage B. The only variable is the encoder init.

Continue MLM on the 20NG train corpus, then fine-tune with Q2b's hyperparameters held verbatim. Anything that moves is the MLM stage.

Stage A · MLM loss

Eval loss ~2.04, perplexity ≈ 7.71. The MLM-eval 90/10 split is internal to the train corpus; the classification val set is never touched.

Stage B · classification confusion (Q2b vs Q3)

Visually almost indistinguishable. DAPT is real but marginal here — the corpus is too small and too close to the English BERT already knows.

Design decisions

  • Independent MLM-eval split. 90/10 carve inside the training docs — the classification val set is held out from MLM exposure.
  • 15 % masking rate, untuned. BERT default. The question was "does DAPT help at all", not "what's the optimal mask rate".
  • Stage B = Q2b hyperparameters, verbatim. Only the encoder init changes.
VI. Question 4 · Llama-3 prompting Section D · Pages 7–8

Llama-3, zero-shot & few-shot.

3 B parameters, frozen, 4-bit. Classification by generated text — parsing is its own diagnostic.

Frozen meta-llama/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct in 4-bit nf4, prompted via chat template. No gradients; output parsed back to a label with substring + Levenshtein fallback.

Demonstrations vs accuracy

k=0 → k=1 → k=3. Few-shot more than doubles zero-shot; even k=3 (60 demos in context) is well below fine-tuned BERT.

VII. Bonus · QLoRA fine-tune Section D · Page 9

QLoRA fine-tune of Llama-3.

Same base as Q4 — this time, allowed to train.

LoRA on {q,k,v,o}_proj; classification head score trained in full precision via modules_to_save. A two-run sweep over rank and LR picks the winner by val macro-F1.

Sweep · validation macro-F1

—

Winner · final test metrics

Evaluated twice: full 7 532-doc test set (vs Q1/Q2/Q3) and the 200-doc subset (byte-identical to Q4).

Per-class delta vs Q4 k=3

Per-class F1 lift on the 200-doc subset. Sibling classes (computer-hardware, religion, politics families) gain the most — exactly where prompting struggled.

Design decisions

  • LoRA on attention projections only. Standard and well-validated locus — rank 16 / 32 specialises attention without retraining 3 B parameters.
  • modules_to_save=['score']. If the freshly-initialised head is wrapped by LoRA, it cannot move enough to learn — loss looks fine, model never classifies. Saving in full precision is the fix.
  • Two evaluations, one winner. Full test → vs Q1/Q2/Q3. Q4 subset → vs zero/few-shot.

IX. Editorial · Lessons learnt Op-Ed · Page 12

Lessons learnt.

Six findings, one consistent surprise. The places parameter count was supposed to substitute for engineering — it didn't.

Seven experiments, one consistent surprise. Parameter count never substituted for engineering; engineering details I'd dismissed as bookkeeping kept deciding the result.

01 · The headline

Representation, not scale, did the work.

110 M parameters of pretrained BERT beat a frozen 3 B Llama-3 by ~35 points on the matched subset. Even QLoRA'd, the 3 B model only edges BERT by ~4 points. Task-specific gradient flow beat both context length and parameter count.

Q2 · BERT Q4 · Llama prompted Q-bonus · QLoRA
02

In-domain MLM is not a free lunch.

Continued MLM lifted Q2b → Q3 by less than one point — well inside seed noise. 11 k documents is too small and too close to generic English. The paper-claim holds at a different order of magnitude.

Q3
03

Classification heads fail quietly.

QLoRA without modules_to_save=['score'] trains, logs sensible loss, and never classifies. The framework gives no warning. Defaults are not safe defaults for novel head wiring.

Q-bonus
04

Free wins exist if you look.

Concatenating max-pool to mean-pool doubled the input with zero new parameters. Engineering can substitute for capacity when the bottleneck is representation, not learning.

Q1d
05

Identical splits, or nothing.

Q1 → Q2 → Q3 lifts mean what they claim only because every stage reuses train_val_split(seed=42). Without that contract, “Q2 beats Q1” is indistinguishable from “Q2 got luckier.”

Disclosure beats laundering.

The Q4 subset is ~5 points easier than the full test set — visible in the gap between Q-bonus full vs subset of the same adapter. Stating it up front protects the comparison.

X. Coming in the next issue Back page · Page 16

Future work.

Four leads, four follow-up experiments. Each one tests a hypothesis raised but not settled by the work in this issue.

Full-test Llama evaluation

Re-run Q4 and the Q-bonus winner over the 7 532-doc test set. Removes the lingering uncertainty around the 200-doc subset.

Larger MLM corpus for Q3

Concatenate 20NG with an external news / forum corpus before the MLM stage. Does the DAPT lift appear once the LM sees new vocabulary?

Instruction-tuned Llama-3.1-8B

The 3 B model already comes within ~5 points of fine-tuned BERT once QLoRA'd. Same training budget, larger model — does the gap close?

Error analysis on sibling classes

Every method's residual error concentrates in the same three clusters (comp.*, politics, religion). A focused study would clarify whether the failure is representational or label-overlap.
